  • Historical Context-The Mary Riley Styles Public Library was founded in 1899 as a private subscription library with financial support from the Falls Church Village Improvement Society (VPIS), which amassed several hundred books for circulation to community subscribers from a structure behind the home of George W. Hawxhurst at the northeast corner of North Washington and East Columbia Streets. In 1913 the collection was transferred from VPIS to the Falls Church Civic League (which became known as the Falls Church Woman's Club in 1916). The Town of Falls Church officially began supporting the library in 1928 with an ordinance establishing the Falls Church Public Library and appropriating $300 for the library. The Woman's Club retained ownership and operation of the Library, which continued to be subscription-based. The Library Committee gifted the library and its holdings to the Town of Falls Church in 1939 (making it a free public library for the first time that year). Upon the death of Mary Riley Styles in 1946, her children bequeathed land from her estate to Falls Church for the eventual construction of a dedicated library building. The building opened its doors to the public at its current location at 120 North Virgina Avenue on July 4, 1958.

  • System Specification-The Mary Riley Styles Public Library's online catalog was launched in 1990. The home page features, at the top, the hours this week link (which you click to get the hours each day of the current week, along with the address, phone number, and a link to view the location on Google Maps); a link to support the library (which takes you to another URL which gives you links to still further URLs that offer you the ability to support the MRSPL Foundation, gives you the guidelines to donate materials to support the library's used book sales, and enables you to find out how you can volunteer with the library); and links you to your account, provided you are a library patron. Below those top three links, all the way to the left of the screen, it offers links to books and more (which offer you the chance to get a card if you don't have one yet); to check out materials (offering you the ability to search the catalog, giving you information about how long you can borrow materials for, detailing what you must do to qualify for an inter-library loan, gives links to eBooks and Audiobooks as well as eMagazines, gives a link to the Spanish language collection, and also gives a link to book club to go kits currently available); and reader resources (which gives links to new books, recommendations for what to read next, the summer reading program, book clubs, recommended reads-which include categories such as America250, Arab American Heritage Moth, Hispanic Heritage Month, Beach Reads, Staff Favorites, Freedom to Read Week, Great Family Read Alouds, and Falls Church History; and the link for you to suggest a title for the library's collection. To the right of books and more is the events tab, which gives you the link to view all events; organizes events by age (preschool, school age, teens, adults, and all ages); and gives a list of featured events (which include storytimes, upcoming book discussions, and ESOL Conversation Groups, as well as the opportunity to propose a program. In the center of the second row is the tab for resources, which features links to review all resources, categorizes resources by type-for kids, teens, or adults-and gives a list of featured resources-among which are Consumer Reports and Thermal Cameras. To the right of resources is the services tab, which allows you to reserve a room; divides services into general services and those for kids, teens, and adults, and gives links to featured services, among which are accessibility, mobile printing, meeting and study rooms, and a link to request exam proctoring. All the way to the right on the second row from the top is the about tab, which gives links to contact info; the our library list, which includes link to hours and location, holidays and closures, policies, staff, library history, and the board of trustees, among other info; and a list of ways to get involved. The center displays the current featured event (hugely prominently) and, the right, a list of events upcoming in the next few days. Below that there are links to services such to get a card, reserve a room, print from anywhere, and local history. Below the prominent center displays are news and notes (which features news such as an upcoming author talk and the fiscal year 2026 annual report for the library. Below the news and notes is the link to the recently added items to the collection. At the very bottom is the contact information and the "connect with us" link to the library's social media accounts.
  • Users:The Mary Riley Stiles Public Library offers you a card if you reside, work or own property in the City of Falls Church, Virginia. You can also get a library card if you reside in: Arlington County, VA; the City of Alexandria, VA; the District of Columbia; Fairfax County, VA; Fauquier County, VA; Frederick County, MD; Loudoun County, VA; Montgomery County, MD; Prince George's County, MD; or Prince William County, VA. For infants ages 0-24 months (with a caregiver-older siblings are welcome), the library offers a baby time, which features a story, songs, and rhymes. For preschoolers (ages 2-4) the library offers playtimes and storytimes. For school age kids (ages 5-11) the library offers such events as arts and crafts events, reading clubs, and math clubs; and services such as Just for Kids(AccessVideo)-a service offering age appropriate educational videos, Brainfuse-a service offering live tutoring homework help, and WritingLab assistance, and Read It-which teaches grammar and reading comprehension for children learning to read, as well as English language learners. For teens the library features teen craft club collages. For adults, the library features events such as FC History room-which are walk-in hours to learn about local history, board game sundays the first Sunday of every month, mindfulness meditation sessions, Tuesday morning book clubs, English conversation classes for ESOL learners, and wellness talks; resources such as Ancestry for Virginia-which enables you to search Virginia-related geneological records, Brainfuse JobNow-which provides resources for job seekers, such as live interview practice, coaching, and resume help, and Brainfuse VetNow-which offers resources for veterans and their families to navigate benefits and services offered by the VA and other organizations.
  • Content: The library offers print books, ebooks, audiobooks, Spanish language materials, magazines, emagazines, newspapers, and DVDs. For books, the catalog provides the title, the author, the publication date, and the genre/Dewewy decimal location. The Mary Riley Styles Public Library has 125,838 total items in the collection and 33,010 total cardholders as of fiscal year 2026.
  • Purpose: The library's mission is supporting community, inspiring discovery, and promoting literacy. Their vision is a sustaianable, informed and caring community of lifelong learners. Their values are welcoming (providing an environment where all are treated with respect and a sense of belonging can be achieved); discovery (offering opportunities to connect, explore new ideas, and expand on all types of literacy); community focus (engaging the community and responding to needs through enhanced relationships, partnerships, and accessible resources); an open mind (maintaining balance in collections, programs, and ideas that respects different viewpoints and allows room for debate and exploration); purpose-driven approach (utilizing data and research in evidence-based decision-making that aligns resources, needs, and support); and flexible and forward-thinking (creating positive interactions by quickly adjusting to expressed needs while anticipating ongoing opportunities to remain future-ready). They are also committed to DEI and customer service.
  • Interesting features and pros: The library website offers a wide variety of services. They go out of their way to make events, the link to the catalog, their news and notes, upcoming events, and other services prominently displayed. They also offer things of substantial value to just about everyone. They have programs that cater extensively to children (including programs aimed specifically at babies and preschoolers), families, adults, ESOL learners, seekers and veterans. The website also prominently displays useful services such as links to help you get a card and reserve a room, as well as resources to study local history.
  • Cons and suggestions for improvement: There were almost no events and services aimed explicitly at teens. Most events and services for teens were lumped in along with those for adults. The library website should offer more events and services aimed explicitly at teens. In addition, the ad display for one featured event (one that is still a few weeks away) was displayed ultra-prominently, to the displacement of all other events. The display for that featured event should not monopolize the screen to the degree that it actually does. Also, the library's contact information is displayed quite obscurely, at the bottom of the screen. It should be displayed in a more prominently visible location. Finally, the library's social media accounts, are also displayed at the bottom of the screen, in even greater obscurity than their contact info. The links to follow the library on social media should be displayed to make them much more easily visible.
