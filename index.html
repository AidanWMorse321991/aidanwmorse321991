Mary Riley Styles Library

  • Context-the Mary Riley Styles Public Library was founded in 1899 as a private subscription library with financial support from the Falls Church Village Improvement Society (VPIS), which amassed several hundred books for circulation to community subscribers from a structure behind the home of George W. Hawxhurst at the northeast corner of North Washington and East Columbia Streets. In 1913 the collection was transferred from VPIS to the Falls Church Civic League (which became known as the Falls Church Woman's Club in 1916). The Town of Falls Church officially began supporting the library in 1928 with an ordinance establishing the Falls Church Public Library and appropriating $300 for the library. The Woman's Club retained ownership and operation of the Library, which continued to be subscription-based. The Library Committee gifted the library and its holdings to the Town of Falls Church in 1939 (making it a free public library for the first time that year). Upon the death of Mary Riley Styles in 1946, her children bequeathed land from her estate to Falls Church for the eventual construction of a dedicated library building. The building opened its doors to the public at its current location at 120 North Virgina Avenue on July 4, 1958. Its online catalog was launched in 1990. It serves Northern Virginia, Washington, D.C. and adjacent parts of Maryland. Their mission is supporting community; inspiring discovery; and promoting literacy. Their vision is a sustainable, informed, and caring community of lifelong learners.

  • Users: The Mary Riley Styles library hosts preschool storytimes for kids ages to 2-4; STEAM-based crafts and activities for kids grades K-5; and book club to-go kits, genealogical services, and board game Sundays for teens and adults.
  • Content: The Mary Riley Stiles Library offers print books, audiobooks, ebooks, and magazines for 21, days all of which are renewable and reservable; DVDs for 7 days which are renewable and reservable; and Lucky Day books for 14 days which are reservable but not renewable.
  • Unique Features and pros: The catalog features a very nice mixture of print books, ebooks, audiobooks, print magazines and e-magazines, as well as an abundance of Spanish-language materials.
  • Cons and suggestions for improvement: The resources and events for teens were lumped in with the events for adults, so it could use more resources and events aimed explicitly at teens.
